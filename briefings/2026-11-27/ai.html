  • Nvidia tells manufacturing partners to pause server shipments to Southeast Asia

    TechCrunch - 26 Nov 2026 20:03 UTC

    Nvidia has asked manufacturing partners, including Dell and Super Micro, to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia for an unspecified period while it assesses whether regional partners meet U.S. export requirements, according to a Wall Street Journal report. The countries are those where Chinese firms have rented remote access to Nvidia chips from local data centres, a route the Commerce Department closed earlier in November; the Institute for Progress estimates such remote capacity supplies roughly 24% of China's AI compute.

  • SAP weighs at least 10,000 job cuts as it reshapes its software business around AI

    CNBC - 27 Nov 2026 06:36 UTC

    Bloomberg reports that SAP is preparing a plan to eliminate at least 10,000 positions while reshaping its software business for the AI era. SAP said the business environment is changing rapidly through AI and that roles and functions will change over time, but that no decisions have been made on further measures. Bloomberg adds that SAP negotiated an employment guarantee with its German works council running through 2028, with product, engineering and sales units expected to take the largest cuts.

  • Chip sellers tell neoclouds to rule out Chinese renters of Nvidia capacity

    CNBC - 26 Nov 2026 15:34 UTC

    Neocloud buyers have been told they cannot purchase Nvidia chips if any of the capacity will be rented by Chinese companies, according to two people familiar with the situation. Chip sellers passed the message on after the Bureau of Industry and Security announced an interim final rule on 16 November restricting remote access to AI semiconductors, which took effect on 19 November; Nvidia had previously said it would not sell to customers renting more than 50% of their GPUs to Chinese companies.

  • C4ADS investigation details China links of US-based Nvidia partners

    SiliconANGLE - 26 Nov 2026 21:13 UTC

    C4ADS, a Washington, D.C.-based nonprofit, carried out an investigation at the request of CBS News into links between companies in Nvidia's supply chain and entities based in China or accused of breaching export controls. The reporting names partners including RiCloud AI and Topmost among the firms examined.

  • Hugging Face publishes a llama.cpp benchmark dashboard for AMD Ryzen and Radeon

    Hugging Face - 26 Nov 2026 11:37 UTC

    The ggml organization published a dashboard that tracks prompt processing and text generation throughput across llama.cpp builds, models and quantizations on AMD Ryzen and Radeon hardware, so that local-inference performance can be compared over time rather than in a single run. The current hardware entries are the Radeon 8060S and the Radeon AI Pro R9700S, and the benchmark workflow and underlying results are public.

  • Running the LTX-2.5 video model on AWS Trainium3

    Hugging Face - 27 Nov 2026 00:00 UTC

    With a month of access to a Trainium3 machine (trn3.48xlarge, 64 NeuronCores) through its AWS partnership, Hugging Face documents what it took to generate video with diffusers on the accelerator, using Lightricks' LTX-2.5 model, which produces video and sound together. Once the models were loaded, a 10-second clip with sound took 22 to 23 seconds to generate on half of the machine.

  • AI agent completes a cardless Papa John's payment in a UK regulatory trial

    Tech.eu - 27 Nov 2026 11:00 UTC

    TrueLayer says an AI agent paid for Papa John's pizzas directly from a customer's bank account in a trial it calls the first time an AI agent has paid a UK retailer this way; the agent helped with the order and then paid from the account linked to the customer's Papa John's account through open banking. The pilot ran inside the Financial Conduct Authority's AI regulatory sandbox, within the limits the customer agreed, and marked the first use of Lloyd's variable recurring payment APIs for agent payments.

  • Portuguese startup Lampsy Health raises €1.1M for a seizure-detecting bedside lamp

    Tech.eu - 27 Nov 2026 08:56 UTC

    Lampsy Health closed a €1.1 million round led by Fundação Ageas and Portugal Ventures, with Angels Way, Phira Ventures and business angels, to fund the commercial launch and distribution in Europe and the UK of a bedside lamp that monitors for epileptic seizures during sleep. The lamp embeds a small camera and a proprietary AI algorithm, detects movements that may indicate a seizure with over 97% accuracy and alerts emergency contacts automatically, without wearable sensors; part of the round comes from the European Innovation Council.
