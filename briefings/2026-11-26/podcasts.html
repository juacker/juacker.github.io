Stories

  • The Revolution vs The Renaissance — Thariq Shihipar, OpenAI Latent Space - 25 Nov 2026 22:28 UTC

    In a talk recorded at AI Engineer CODE, OpenAI's Thariq Shihipar argues that cheap, model-generated software and small-batch work that has to feel right can coexist rather than compete. The episode is built around a fighting game in which he edits the size of a rock.

  • If AI Can Pass the Exam, What Should College Test For? with Suzy Siegle The Artificial Intelligence Podcast - 25 Nov 2026 14:00 UTC

    Jonathan Green interviews Walsh College president Suzy Siegle on what universities should assess once models can pass exams: critical thinking, problem solving and grit rather than lecture content, with lecture material turned self-serve and the live session kept for argument and connection. She also describes the entry-level rung of the career ladder as the first thing to break.

  • Responding to objections to recursive self-improvement Dwarkesh Podcast - 24 Nov 2026 21:45 UTC

    Dwarkesh Patel answers objections to his essay arguing that recursive self-improvement leads to superintelligence by 2030, working through the strongest counterarguments with his general manager Max Farrens. The episode is framed around what the skeptics get right.

  • Warp bets on the software factory Changelog Interviews #693 - 24 Nov 2026 20:00 UTC

    Warp founder Zach Lloyd presents Warp Factories, open infrastructure he bills as the successor to CI/CD for agent-driven development, covering factory-as-code, what an agent trace contains and why self-improving systems drift. He also describes how Warp's senior engineers moved from guarding code to being its heaviest adopters once the models were good enough.

  • What Should a Lab Do Before It Feeds Its Data to AI? with Loren Perelman The Artificial Intelligence Podcast - 24 Nov 2026 14:00 UTC

    Loren Perelman of Zontal argues that laboratory and industrial data has to be gathered, categorized and modelled before it reaches a model, because a model fed unordered data forms connections that do not hold. The episode sets out the order of operations - collect, classify, drop what does not matter, then model - and notes that fear of replacement stalls the projects that would carry the value.
